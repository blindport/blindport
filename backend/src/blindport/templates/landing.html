Blindport Relay HTTPS

Automatic HTTPS, local app port

After you accept the Let's Encrypt agreement, blindportd obtains and renews the certificate, handles HTTPS on your host, and forwards plaintext to your app at a local target such as 127.0.0.1:8080.

Advanced TLS passthrough remains available when your existing server must keep the certificate and terminate TLS itself.

Running the ingress yourself?

Blindport is also a fully MIT-licensed, self-hostable stack, not only a hosted service. It can be more capable than a simple tunnel relay when you need durable allocation state, an exact TCP or UDP socket, or a routed public IPv4 /32. Self-hosting means you operate availability, abuse controls, DNS, certificates, payments, and network inventory.

Privacy boundaries

    
Blindport does not expose the origin IP through its network path and does not require a name or identity profile. Applications, historical DNS, headers, or third-party integrations can still reveal an origin.

    
Blindport processes source addresses transiently for routing and active abuse controls, but does not persist visitor or request source IPs in application records. Hosted access logging is disabled, and operational logs omit those source addresses and are deleted within 30 days.

    
Blindport retains allocated endpoint addresses, domains, account activity times, payment records, and tunnel state needed to provide the service. It can observe destination ports or SNI, timing, and volume while traffic is active. HTTP-01 handling also parses the bounded Host, path, query, and headers. Plaintext TCP or UDP is not made private by forwarding it.

    
Blindport is not an anonymity network. You can route blindportd outbound connections through a Tor SOCKS5 proxy, or run the client on a VPS, according to your threat model. Those choices change the visible client network path, but they do not make the service anonymous.
