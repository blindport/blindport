Plain-language beta terms

Service terms

Blindport provides public ingress for lawful services running on hardware you control. By creating an order or operating an endpoint, you agree to these terms.

Effective August 4, 2026

Intended use

Blindport is built for self-hosters who want easier public reachability and less exposure of their origin address to ordinary visitors and internet scans. Blindport's network path does not disclose the origin address to them, but application behavior, DNS history, headers, or third-party integrations may still reveal it. The application, data, and TLS termination can remain on your hardware while Blindport supplies the public network path.

You are responsible for the service behind your endpoint, its security, its users, and the content or activity it makes available.

Best-effort beta

The hosted beta is provided on a best-effort basis without an uptime, availability, or high-availability guarantee. High availability is planned after beta, but planned work is not a promise about the current service.

Traffic and fair use

There is currently no fixed traffic limit. This is a fair-use service, not a promise of unlimited capacity.

We may introduce reasonable traffic, bandwidth, connection, or rate limits when needed to keep the service reliable and available. We will publish material limits before they apply when practical. Urgent limits may be applied immediately to contain abuse, instability, security incidents, or harm to other users.

Source addresses and logs

Blindport does not persist visitor or request source IP addresses in application records. Source addresses are processed transiently where required to route active traffic, apply in-memory abuse controls, and carry connection metadata to customer infrastructure. Allocated service addresses, domains, subscriptions, account activity times, and payment records are retained as necessary to provide the service.

Hosted access logging is disabled. Blindport operational logs omit visitor and request source addresses and are deleted within 30 days. Customer systems and independent network, hosting, DNS, certificate, payment, email, and monitoring providers are outside this retention commitment and may keep their own records.

Lawful use and upstream providers

You may not use a Blindport IP, Port, or Relay endpoint for content or activity that violates laws applicable to you, Blindport, or the infrastructure used to provide the service.

Blindport depends on network, hosting, DNS, and payment providers. We cannot prevent those providers from investigating use, filtering traffic, or suspending their services or Blindport infrastructure. We may suspend or remove an endpoint when we reasonably believe it is being used unlawfully, when an upstream provider requires action, or when continued operation threatens the service's security, reliability, or availability.

An endpoint removed for illegal content or activity is not eligible for a refund.

Privacy and threat model

Blindport is privacy-enhancing ingress, not an anonymity network or censorship-resistance system. Blindport and its providers can observe connection metadata such as addresses, ports or SNI hostnames, timing, and traffic volume. Payloads are opaque to Blindport only when your service uses suitable end-to-end encryption.

This architecture is not designed to resist a state-level adversary, targeted traffic correlation, legal compulsion, infrastructure seizure, or coordinated pressure on upstream providers. It is intended for ordinary lawful self-hosting where origin-address privacy and ease of use matter.

Operators who require anonymity or censorship resistance should evaluate tools designed for those goals, such as Tor onion services, suitable mixnets, or other specialized systems, against their exact threat model. We support the broader goals of privacy, anonymity, and censorship resistance; Blindport itself provides only the limited privacy benefits described above.

Changes

These beta terms may evolve as the service and its operating constraints become clearer. Material changes will be published on this page with a new effective date.
